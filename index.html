What we're building

Our current focus is a memory-aid and escalating-response system — software that helps someone know where they are, what's happening around them, and what to do next, without needing a live internet connection.

It's designed first for people managing real cognitive challenges day to day, and built by people who understand the need for up-to-date, accurate information at the moment it matters most.

That understanding is first-hand, not theoretical.

Our first tool: Treo

Treo is a simple orientation aid that runs on a phone. When things feel confusing, one big button shows where you are, what is around you, and what is most likely next, using your own saved places, calendar and the time of day.

Treo offers a gentle guess, such as “Are you going to the pharmacy?” A Yes or No lets you confirm or correct it. If the first guess is wrong, it moves on to the next most likely one.

Your saved places, contacts and events stay on your own phone, not on our servers, and there is no account to create. Treo is an early test version and is not a safety or medical device, so please don't rely on it for anything urgent.

About

About us

Oakes Development Studio LLC is a small, independent studio in Santa Cruz, California. It was formed in July 2026 to build simple tools for people with brain injuries and for the people who support them.

We have learned that the tools that last are the simple ones, built around one person's real day. We build slowly, and we test with the people who will use them.

We want to shape what comes next with people who live with this every day, and with the programs that know them best.

About me

I'm Patrick Oakes Halpin, the founder. I live with acquired brain injuries affecting the frontal cortex, and I have been in active rehabilitation for more than twenty years.

My rehab has been practical and slow. It started with post-it notes on the kitchen cabinets and the bathroom mirror: turn off the oven, brush your teeth, take your medication. Later I moved to a computer and speech-to-text, and now to my phone.

I built the first version of Treo for myself. Everything we build starts from the same question: what actually helps on an ordinary day?
